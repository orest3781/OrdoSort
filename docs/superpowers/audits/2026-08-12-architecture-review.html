Two cards were wrong in ways worth keeping visible rather than editing away. Problem

The subtlest safety rule in the codebase — write to a temp sibling, retry on transient lock, and only clean up a partial write if this call created it — is re-derived in five implementations.

Solution

One module owns temp-sibling placement, the retry budget, and the created-by-me callback. Callers supply only the write action and replace-vs-create semantics.

Correction — this card fused two different disciplines

It claimed five sites share one idiom. They don't, and the diagram above is wrong about it. Config's two have no created-by-me gate at all — and correctly so: their temp name carries a GUID, so no peer can own it, cleanup is unconditional and the destination is never touched. Unlock, ZipMerge and Zipper's default branch have no temp sibling — they write straight to a collision-freed name a peer legitimately can own, which is exactly why they need a gate. Zipper.ExtractCore writes a directory.

Two rules that read alike because their doc comments cross-reference each other — and I read the comments, not the code. Scope was cut from five sites to three before anything was built: the ones that genuinely do temp→final placement. The other three keep their gates untouched.

Deletion test, as it actually applies

Delete Zipper.PlaceAtomically and its retry loop does not vanish — it must be retyped, as it already had been from Config.WriteAtomic, whose own doc comment admitted it: "same shape, same reasoning." That part held. What did not hold was extending the same argument to the created-gate sites, where deleting the gate would delete a real safety property rather than a duplicate.

Outcome

AtomicPlace with two entry points — TryReplace and TryCreateNew — over one implementation and one seam. Three call sites migrated one per commit. Zip Save-As gained its first-ever retry test: the loop existed, was byte-identical to a proven one, and nothing anywhere proved it worked. CONTEXT.md now records why atomic placement and the created-by-me gate must not be merged, so the next reviewer doesn't repeat this card's mistake.

  • Locality: the shared-folder race in one module
  • Two seams collapse, not four
  • Retry budget proved once, for three sites
  • The two disciplines now visibly differ

Problem

Two ViewModels call Task.Run directly, so their tests cannot swap in the inline adapter and must synchronise against real threads instead.

Solution

Take IWorkScheduler in both constructors and route the five call sites through it, matching the other twelve.

Correction — this card originally overstated its case

It claimed three 2026-08-10 commits fought flakiness "caused by exactly this gap." Reading 41ae2f7 rather than a summary of it, that is wrong: the race was a 2000ms start deadline, fixed by replacing it with unbounded test-owned gates. The same commit deliberately removed a production seam — "This also removes the OffUiThreadDispatchForTests production seam the last attempt added, taking the seam count back down." The team was reducing seam count, not missing one.

The gap is real — 12 of 14 is a genuine inconsistency — but the commit history is not evidence for it, and treating it as such is the error. Downgraded from Strong to Worth exploring accordingly.

What shipping it actually showed

The ManualResetEventSlim gating turned out not to be a workaround for the missing seam at all: all four sites exist to observe a probe that is genuinely in flight, which InlineWorkScheduler would erase rather than simplify. A third double — one that queues work instead of running it — removed the gating without hollowing the tests out, verified by mutating each guard and confirming only the right tests failed. So the seam did pay off in the test surface, just not for the reason this card gave.

What is already deep

Stated because it explains why the candidates above stand out: the friction here is narrow and idiom-specific, not systemic.

Dialogs and dispatch

Zero direct MessageBox / OpenFileDialog / Dispatcher.Invoke in either 2000-line ViewModel — all behind IDialogService and SynchronizationContext. Large ≠ shallow.

Coverage

All 28 Core modules have direct or clearly attributable tests; 482 facts in Core.Tests. Scanner and ConfigDocs looked untested by filename and were verified covered.

The E2E suite

Not compensating for untestable modules — it proves real ViewModel-to-Core wiring through a real window, which neither tier covers. Its plan states the rule explicitly.

Naming, Commit, DebouncedProbe

Naming is filesystem-free by design; Commit absorbs the move-and-retry ordering rather than pushing it onto Session; DebouncedProbe is one mechanism reused correctly.

Start here

Candidate 2 first, then candidate 4

Candidate 2 is the only one on this page that is producing a wrong result today: two of nine intake modules dedupe case-sensitively, and they are precisely the two that drive File.Move. A case-only duplicate becomes a second row, and the second File.Move fails on a file the first already moved — surfacing to the user as a per-file failure that has no per-file cause. Fixing the two call sites is minutes; deepening intake so a tenth tool cannot drift again is the real work.

Then candidate 4 — the cheapest diff here. The seam exists and twelve of fourteen ViewModels use it, so the two that don't are a plain inconsistency. Note the correction on that card: I originally cited three flaky-test commits as proof of what the gap had cost, and that reading does not survive contact with the commits themselves. Take it as a small consistency win, not a flake fix.

Candidates 1, 3 and 5 are the same story at three scales — an idiom with no single owner. Candidate 3 is the one to take seriously: it is the highest-stakes rule in the codebase and the most re-derived, and every copy carries a doc comment naming the sibling it was copied from.
